<p><strong>1. Could you briefly introduce yourself?</strong></p><p><br>My name is Ikrame, and I graduated from École Centrale de Nantes, where I followed a general engineering track. Before that, I spent two years in preparatory classes at the Mohammed VI Excellence High School, a demanding experience that prepared me for the competitive entrance exams to the Grandes Écoles. After a great deal of hard work and perseverance, I was fortunate enough to get into one of the schools of the Centrale group. Today, I work in the digital field on projects for EDF, in a stimulating and meaningful environment.</p><p><strong>2. Why did you choose to study in France?</strong></p><p><br>My choice was driven above all by the quality of the training and the academic excellence of French engineering schools, particularly those of the Centrale group. These institutions offer rigorous teaching, an international outlook and a hands-on approach to engineering. For me, studying at École Centrale de Nantes was an opportunity to grow in a demanding, diverse and innovation-driven setting.</p><p><strong>3. Where did you study in France, and how did you settle in?</strong></p><p><br>I studied at École Centrale de Nantes. Arriving in France was a milestone, full of adjustments and discoveries. It was my first time living abroad, and I had to learn to become fully independent. This stage enriched me deeply: it made me more responsible, better organized and more confident, both personally and professionally.</p><p><strong>4. Did you notice differences between the French and Moroccan education systems?</strong></p><p><br>Yes, there are differences between the two systems. In France, there is a stronger emphasis on autonomy and practical projects, often in connection with companies, which makes the training more engaging and career-oriented. The Moroccan system, which is more structured and theoretical, is nonetheless changing quickly and increasingly incorporating practical approaches. In the end, the two models complement each other: one builds rigor, the other openness and initiative.</p><p><strong>5. Did you join any student associations, clubs or networks?</strong></p><p><br>I wasn't able to get fully involved in student associations or clubs, largely because of the Covid situation. On the other hand, I was lucky to meet people from all over the world and to build strong bonds with my classmates, in France and in Morocco, which helped me develop a valuable network, both personally and professionally. Alongside my studies, I volunteered as a leader for young children in the Scouts, a rewarding experience that helped me integrate and develop my communication and leadership skills.</p><p><strong>6. What were the most difficult moments of your journey?</strong></p><p><br>One of the biggest challenges was adapting to a new pace of life and work. Moving from preparatory classes to the engineering program required real self-reflection: the way of learning, project management, personal organization… Everything was different. Finding internships, especially abroad, was not easy either. But these difficult moments taught me a lot; they made me more resilient, more independent and, above all, more confident in my abilities.</p><p><strong>7. What was the most important thing you learned during your time in France?</strong></p><p><br>The most important lesson was learning to trust myself. I learned to stop underestimating myself, to dare, to claim my place and to believe in my skills. Studying at École Centrale, in such a competitive and diverse environment, allowed me to meet people from different cultures and to grow through these exchanges. This experience helped me open up, develop and assert my legitimacy.</p><p><br><strong>8. Did you take part in any projects, internships or formative experiences?</strong></p><p><br>Yes, I was fortunate to complete several internships in a variety of fields, including one abroad at a multinational company. That experience gave me a great deal: learning to settle into a new country, to work with multicultural teams and to better understand how the professional world operates. These experiences helped me gain confidence, openness and adaptability.</p><p><strong>9. Was the scholarship a driving force in your journey?</strong></p><p><br>Absolutely. The FIRSI scholarship was a real driving force in my journey. It gave me the peace of mind I needed to focus fully on my studies without worrying about financial constraints. Beyond the material help, it also taught me to be responsible and to manage my personal finances better. It was genuine support, both moral and practical.</p><p><strong>10. If you had to sum up your experience in one sentence or one word, what would it be?</strong></p><p><br>I would describe my experience as a transformative journey, both personal and professional. Studying in France pushed me to surpass myself, take on responsibilities and learn to overcome difficulties. I gained confidence, openness and maturity. I also learned to be more sociable and to communicate with people of very different ages, cultures and backgrounds, an invaluable human wealth.</p><p><strong>11. Have you gone through any kind of career change?</strong></p><p><br>No, I haven't changed careers as such; it has been more of a natural evolution of my path. My journey gradually led me toward digital technology and information systems, a field in which I feel completely at home today.</p><p><strong>12. Where do you see yourself in five years?</strong></p><p><br>In five years, I see myself continuing to grow in the tech field, in positions with greater responsibility. I want to keep proving my worth, challenging myself and learning. And in the longer term, I would like to return to my country to create my own start-up, a project that would combine my technical skills with my desire to contribute to the development and success of young Moroccan talent.</p>
