<p><strong>1. </strong><em><strong>Could you briefly introduce yourself?</strong></em></p><p>My name is Nouhaila AGOUZAL, and I am 24 years old. After earning my baccalaureate at Lycée Mohammed VI in Marrakech, I entered the preparatory classes at the Mohammed VI Excellence High School in Benguerir, and then Grenoble INP – ENSIMAG in Grenoble, France, where I trained in computer science and applied mathematics. I then broadened my horizons through an Erasmus exchange at KTH Royal Institute of Technology in Stockholm, Sweden. Today, I am a machine learning engineer at Dassault Systèmes, a French group specializing in 3D design, simulation, and manufacturing software.</p><p><em><strong>2. Why did you choose to study in France?</strong></em></p><p>Choosing to study in France was both an ambition and a challenge. Leaving Morocco meant moving away from my parents and everything familiar, and going forward with the fear of cultural differences and loneliness. It was not an obvious decision, especially when you come from a modest family background where every distance is felt more keenly. Yet that fear was not stronger than my desire to learn and to push myself. </p><p>After preparatory classes, I felt the need to train in a highly demanding environment, one that could nurture my passion for computer science and push me to the highest level. France emerged as the logical next step: a system renowned for its academic excellence and the quality of its engineering schools, offering an education that is rigorous, internationally minded, and accessible.</p><p><em><strong>3. Where did you study in France, and how did your arrival go?</strong></em></p><p>I studied in Grenoble, at Grenoble INP – ENSIMAG. My arrival was a defining moment: settling into a new country while handling administrative procedures and a demanding study schedule was not easy. Yet from the very first days, I felt supported. The Moroccan community there helped me fit in by offering practical advice, guidance about the school, and above all, real moral support. Thanks to this solidarity, my adjustment went more smoothly.</p><p><strong>4. Did you notice any differences between the French and Moroccan education systems?</strong></p><p>From what I have observed, the differences between the Moroccan and French education systems lie mainly in <strong>the teaching approach,</strong> rather than in the academic level itself.</p><p>The Moroccan system places strong emphasis on <strong>scientific rigor</strong>. The education is highly structured, demanding, and focused on acquiring solid theoretical foundations. This approach develops strong reasoning skills, discipline, and a capacity for hard work, but sometimes leaves less room for experimentation, autonomy, or applied projects.</p><p>In France, particularly in engineering schools, teaching is more <strong>geared toward practice and the concrete application</strong> of knowledge. Projects, group work, case studies, and links with the professional world play a major role. Greater value is placed on personal initiative, communication, teamwork, and the ability to solve real-world problems.</p><p><strong>5. </strong><strong>Did you join any student associations, clubs, or networks?</strong></p><p>Yes. During my studies, I chose to get involved in several associations, wanting to be useful, to build connections, and to learn to make a voice heard, both my own and that of others. This involvement was also a valuable school for developing essential skills, particularly in communication, teamwork, and taking initiative. I was first a member of <strong>ENS’IA</strong>, an association that promotes artificial intelligence at Grenoble INP – ENSIMAG. I also joined <strong>IAESTE France (Grenoble Alpes)</strong>, the International Association for the Exchange of Students for Technical Experience, where I helped organize events and workshops. Finally, I joined <strong>EMF Paris-Saclay</strong>, an association dedicated to serving, representing, and defending students.</p><p><strong>6. What were the most difficult moments of your journey?</strong></p><p>My journey was shaped by several demanding transitions, each of which contributed to my personal and academic growth. First, the move from preparatory classes to the engineering program required real adjustment: a new way of working, more projects, and greater autonomy. I was also faced with very high expectations in computer science, which pushed me to redouble my efforts to progress quickly.</p><p>At the same time, I had to deal with very practical difficulties related to administrative procedures, first in France and then in Sweden, as well as the preparation for each move, which was often a source of stress. Finally, the most delicate moments were those when I had to choose a direction: a track, an internship, an exchange destination. At those times, at an age when you still lack perspective on your aspirations, being able to talk with sincere people who were willing to share their experience and give straightforward guidance was essential support that helped me move forward with greater confidence.</p><p><strong>7. What was the most significant thing you learned during your time in France?</strong></p><p>During my time in France, the most significant lessons were as much human as academic. I realized that everyone starts from a different place: there is no point in comparing yourself to others, only to the person you were yesterday. This perspective taught me to honor my journey and to recognize, with pride, everything I had to overcome and build to become the person I am today. </p><p>It also taught me resilience and perseverance in the face of failure: life is full of trials, but I understood that you can go through them without losing yourself, by relying on faith and trust in God. It also helped me better manage stress and loneliness in difficult times and, above all, learn to listen to myself and love myself more. Finally, it taught me that you can open up to others and learn from them without losing yourself or turning your back on your culture.</p><p><em><strong>8. Did you carry out any projects, internships, or formative experiences?</strong></em></p><p>Yes, I carried out several particularly formative projects and internships at the intersection of software development, data science, and artificial intelligence, which allowed me to consolidate my skills while gaining professional maturity. These included an experience in data science and natural language processing at <strong>Ivalua,</strong> another in an international research setting at the <strong>ILL – Institut Laue-Langevin</strong>, where I contributed to the development of the open-source software SasView, and an academic project with <strong>Atos</strong> on AI applied to images.</p><p>These experiences took place in international, multicultural environments, where I strengthened my adaptability, communication, and collaboration skills.</p><p><strong>9. Was the scholarship a lever in your journey?</strong></p><p>Absolutely. Without the scholarship, I would not have been able to pursue my plan to study in France. Coming from a modest family, this support was decisive in funding my move and covering the essential costs of student life.</p><p>Beyond the financial aspect, the scholarship gave me invaluable stability, allowing me to devote myself fully to my studies and move forward with peace of mind.</p><p><strong>10. If you had to sum up your experience in one sentence or one word, what would it be?</strong></p><p>A path of pushing my limits and resilience, with my values as my compass.</p><p><strong>11. Have you experienced any kind of career change? </strong></p><p>No, I did not have to change careers, because my path has remained consistent and aligned with my early interest in science, and then in computer science. On the contrary, each stage of my education strengthened this choice and allowed me to gradually refine my career plans, leading to my current position as a machine learning engineer.</p><p><strong>12. </strong><strong>Where do you see yourself in five years?</strong></p><p>I do not plan my future five years ahead in great detail, because I prefer to build my path step by step and stay open to the opportunities that arise along the way. That said, even without a fixed plan, I have a direction that is deeply important to me.</p><p>Ideally, I see myself in Morocco, where I could put my skills to work for concrete, useful projects. I would like to contribute to my country's development, help spread science and technology, and work, on my own scale, to make these opportunities more accessible. I would also like to get involved in passing on knowledge and mentoring, supporting young people — especially those from modest backgrounds — by sharing my experience, advising them, and, where possible, opening doors for them, as others did for me.</p><p><em>Thank you for sharing your story! </em></p><p><br> </p>
