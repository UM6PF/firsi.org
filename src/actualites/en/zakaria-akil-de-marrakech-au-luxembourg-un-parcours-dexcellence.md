<p><strong>Could you briefly introduce yourself?</strong></p><p>My name is Zakaria Akil. I completed my secondary education at Hassan II High School in Marrakech, before attending preparatory classes at the Lycée d'Excellence in Benguerir (Lydex - UM6P). I then joined Télécom Paris to specialize in Signal Processing and Computer Vision, followed by École Polytechnique for a second-year Master's (M2) in Data Science. I am currently an intern at Amazon in Luxembourg.</p><p><strong>Why did you choose to study in France?</strong></p><p>After preparatory classes, it is quite natural for many Moroccan students to pursue higher education in France. It is an accessible, well-recognized path that offers academic excellence at the European and international level.</p><p><strong>Where did you study in France, and how did you settle in?</strong></p><p>I studied at Télécom Paris for my engineering degree, then at École Polytechnique for the M2 Master's in Data Science. Settling in went very well: the transition was smooth, I quickly found my bearings and felt at ease both in the academic environment and in everyday life.</p><p><strong>Did you notice differences between the French and Moroccan education systems?</strong></p><p>I didn't pursue higher education in Morocco, so I can't really compare the two systems. However, compared with my years in preparatory classes at Lydex, I found the pace and the expectations fairly well aligned, with a logical continuity in scientific training.</p><p><strong>Did you join any student associations, clubs or networks?</strong></p><p>Yes, I was lucky to get involved in student life. In particular, I joined the Sports Office, where I was co-president of the soccer team. These experiences helped me develop interpersonal and organizational skills.</p><p><strong>What were the most difficult moments of your journey?</strong></p><p>Exam periods at Télécom Paris were probably the most demanding. The workload was quite intense and required rigorous organization.</p><p><strong>What was the most important thing you learned during your time in France?</strong></p><p>Beyond academic knowledge, I mainly learned to develop real autonomy, to work in multicultural environments and to adapt quickly to new contexts.</p><p><strong>Did you take part in any projects, internships or formative experiences?</strong></p><p>Yes, I had the opportunity to take part in many projects, both academic and extracurricular. I also completed two internships abroad, which were extremely formative and allowed me to strengthen my technical skills while discovering new professional environments.</p><p><strong>Was the scholarship a driving force in your journey?</strong></p><p>Yes, the financial support made things easier, as it allowed me to focus fully on my studies and projects without excessive financial constraints.</p><p><strong>If you had to sum up your experience in one sentence or one word, what would it be?</strong></p><p>An enriching experience, marked by discovery, learning and meeting people.</p><p><strong>Have you gone through any kind of career change? </strong></p><p>Not really. My path has been fairly linear so far, but I have always sought to broaden my horizons and explore new areas related to applied mathematics and computer science.</p><p><strong>Where do you see yourself in five years?</strong></p><p>I'm not in the habit of drawing up a rigid plan for the future, because I strongly believe in the opportunities that arise along the way. That said, in five years, I see myself in an international setting, driven by innovation and challenge, where I can keep growing and contribute to impactful projects.</p>
